1 What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack where criminals pretend to be a trusted person or organisation — your bank, PayPal, Google, Netflix, or even your boss — to trick you into revealing sensitive information like passwords, OTPs, credit card numbers, or Aadhaar/PAN details.

The word "phishing" comes from "fishing" — attackers throw out bait (fake emails, fake websites, fake messages) and wait for victims to "bite" by clicking a link or entering credentials.

🎯 Goal of every phishing attack: Steal your login credentials, money, or identity. That's it.

Unlike malware that exploits software bugs, phishing exploits human psychology — urgency, fear, greed, curiosity. That's why it's still so effective even in 2025.

2 Types of Phishing Attacks

Email Phishing

The classic — fake email from "your bank"

Mass emails impersonating trusted brands. They contain a link to a fake login page that steals your password the moment you type it.

Example: "Your account is suspended — click here to verify now."

Spear Phishing

Targeted at a specific person

Attackers research you on LinkedIn / social media, then send a personalised email that looks 100% legitimate — often impersonating your boss or colleague.

Example: "Hi Riya, can you urgently share the client database? — CEO"

Smishing (SMS Phishing)

Fake SMS with a link

Text messages pretending to be from your bank, courier, or government. Common in India: "Your KYC is expiring, click to update."

Example: "Dear user, your SBI account will be blocked. Update KYC: bit.ly/kyc-update"

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

A phone call from a "bank officer"

Attackers call you pretending to be from your bank, RBI, or police, and pressure you to share OTP or install a "support app" (which is malware).

Example: "Sir, your card is being misused. Please share the OTP to block it."

Website Spoofing

Fake login pages that look real

A pixel-perfect copy of paypal.com or icicibank.com on a lookalike domain like paypal-secure.xyz. You enter credentials — they're stolen instantly.

Example: https://secure-login-verify.xyz (not the real PayPal)

Quishing (QR Phishing)

Malicious QR codes — newest trend

Fake QR codes on posters, restaurant tables, or parking meters that redirect to a phishing page. Very common in India post-UPI.

Example: Scan QR to "pay parking fee" — actually sends money to scammer.

Whaling

Targeting CEOs and executives

Highly sophisticated attacks aimed at senior leaders — often using fake legal notices, board meeting invites, or M&A documents.

Example: Fake "SEBI notice" sent to a company director.

Clone Phishing

A copy of a legit email you already got

Attacker takes a real email you received earlier, replaces the link/attachment with a malicious one, and resends it — making it look familiar.

Example: "Resending the invoice as promised" with a fake attachment.
3 6 Red Flags of a Phishing Attack

Urgency & Fear

"Act within 24 hours or your account will be closed!" — real companies never pressure you like this.

Suspicious Links

Hover over a link — if the domain doesn't match the brand (e.g. paypal.secure-login.xyz), it's phishing.

Wrong Sender Address

"support@paypa1.com" (with a number 1) instead of "@paypal.com". Always check the full email ID.

Grammar & Spelling Errors

Real companies proofread. "Dear Valued Costumer, your acount..." = red flag.

Asks for Sensitive Info

No bank, UPI, or government agency ever asks for OTP, PIN, CVV, or password over email, SMS, or phone. Ever.

Unexpected Attachments

Invoices you didn't order, .zip files, or .exe attachments are often malware. Don't open them.

5 How to Stay Safe (Prevention Tips)

Check Before You Click

Hover over every link. Verify the domain matches the real brand. When in doubt, type the site manually.

Enable 2FA / MFA

Even if attackers steal your password, they can't login without the second factor (OTP app / hardware key).

Use a Password Manager

It auto-fills passwords only on the real website — so you'll never type your password into a fake page.

Never Share OTP / PIN

No bank, UPI app, or government official will ever ask for your OTP, PIN, or CVV. Hang up immediately.

Use PhishShield AI

Paste suspicious emails, URLs, or messages into our scanner before clicking. Get an instant risk score.

Report & Report Fast

Report phishing to your IT team or at cybercrime.gov.in — the official Indian government portal.
